# Only <em>7</em> in every 1,000 uranium atoms can easily split

Uranium dug out of the ground is a mix of two isotopes. They behave the same chemically and differ by just three neutrons, but only one of them matters for most reactors. **U-235** splits when hit by a slow neutron and releases energy. **U-238**, almost all of the rest, mostly does not.

**Enrichment** is the process of raising the share of U-235. Each grid below is a sample of 1,000 atoms. Compare how many are orange.

## Notes and method

Natural uranium is 99.27% U-238, 0.72% U-235 and 0.005% U-234 by atom count. U-234 is too rare to show (about 1 atom in 20,000).

Enrichment levels are quoted by mass, as the industry does. U-235 is slightly lighter, so 5% and 90% by mass correspond to about 51 and 901 atoms per 1,000; the grids round to 50 and 900. "Low-enriched" means below 20% U-235 by mass; above that it is "highly enriched".

Sources: [IUPAC CIAAW, uranium isotopic abundances](https://www.ciaaw.org/uranium.htm); atomic masses from AME2020; IAEA Safeguards Glossary. The numbers are computed in `analysis/uranium-enrichment/build_data.py`.
